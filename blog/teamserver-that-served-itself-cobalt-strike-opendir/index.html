    The Teamserver That Served Itself: Reading a Cobalt Strike Operator's Own C Drive¶

    Summary¶

    In November 2026 I came across an open directory on 45.251.240[.]174:8080 most likely published by a Chinese-speaking e-crime operator who had bound a Python http.server to the root of their own Windows C drive. The directory served the complete working set of a live Cobalt Strike 4.9 teamserver, including its data/ subdirectory with the serialised beacon, session, archive, listener, and target databases. Reading those files from the outside, gave me a complete inventory of the operator's arsenal. One of those beacons sits on a Microsoft IIS 10 server fronting a Vietnamese government survey platform. This note is the technical walkthrough of how I read each teamserver database, how I decoded the beacon metadata, and how I independently confirmed the Vietnamese government compromise before notifying VNCERT.

    Background¶

    Cobalt Strike is a commercial red-team platform sold by Fortra, and the single most common post-exploitation framework in both state-nexus and criminal intrusions worldwide.1 Cracked builds circulate widely, and the specific image this operator runs, Cobalt Strike 4.9 released October 2023, has been mirrored on Chinese security forums since shortly after its release. The teamserver component (the Java process the operator connects to from the client GUI) holds its complete working memory (beacons, sessions, listeners, archives, task output) in a data/ subdirectory next to the installation. Anyone who can read those files reads everything the operator sees.

    Windows Subsystem for Linux mounts the Windows filesystem under /mnt/c, and under an elevated shell gives Linux processes NTFS read against it. Python's built-in http.server auto-indexes every directory under its root. Binding one to the other, as this operator did, publishes the entire C drive as a clickable directory listing. The teamserver's data/ happens to sit under that root, which is why the rest of this note is possible.

    The target set is consistent with the Chinese-language 黑灰产 (hēihuīchǎn, "black-grey industry") ecosystem that runs illegal online gambling and the associated cash-out operations. The operator's own archive is literally named ARTEX菠菜专武（带武器库）.7z, "ARTEX gambling-industry special-weapon (with weapons library)", where 菠菜 (bōcài, "spinach") is the standard slang homophone for 博彩 (bócǎi, "gambling") used across these forums. This is e-crime infrastructure, not state-nexus.

    The Files That Make Up a Cobalt Strike Teamserver's Memory¶

    The opendir was created by a single operator script, rep8080[.]sh, that bound Python's http.server to the WSL mount /mnt/c:

    #!/bin/bash
pkill -f "http.server" 2>/dev/null
setsid nohup python3 -m http.server 8080 --directory /mnt/c >/dev/null 2>&1 < /dev/null &
sleep 2
ss -tln 2>/dev/null | grep 8080 || netstat -tln 2>/dev/null | grep 8080
echo done

    Because WSL under an elevated shell gives a Linux process Administrator-level NTFS read, and because python -m http.server auto-indexes every directory under its root while following reparse points, binding to /mnt/c published the entire Windows C drive on port 8080 with a clickable directory listing. The operator probably wanted a quick way to serve freshly-generated beacons out of C:\qlkh.elf, C:\beacon.dll, or similar, and ended up with a full file-browser of their own operational workbench.

    The part of that workbench that matters here is the Cobalt Strike installation at OneCS/OneCS-49_尊享版/, and inside it the data/ subdirectory where CS 4.9 persists everything the operator sees in the client GUI.

    addnotes.cna: The Operator's Own Compromise Inventory¶

    The single clearest piece of attribution and intent on the whole box is a Cobalt Strike Aggressor script called addnotes.cna. It documents every active beacon in the operator's teamserver roster with a short reminder of where they got in, what webshell lets them back in, and where the beacon lives on disk. It lives at the root of the opendir, served as plain text.

    one.profile: The Malleable C2 Fingerprint¶

    Any Cobalt Strike teamserver is only as unique as its malleable C2 profile, and this one is in the clear at OneCS-49_尊享版/one.profile. The fingerprint card:

    Beacon callbacks go to /api/v1/get for check-in and /api/v1/post for data. The SSL certificate is self-signed with subject CN=Google Spider, O=Alphabet Inc, L=Sacramento, ST=California, C=US and SHA-256 fingerprint 5f217b0f96c1e5f32a796dde0de01e87c30ff52ec343636c08a7d2efee8ab405. Every HTTP response on an unknown URI carries Server: nginx and the beacon callback's Accept-Language header is hard-coded to zh-CN,zh;q=0.9,en;q=0.8. The last one is the hunt signal worth emphasising: a request from any workstation whose users are not Chinese-reading, carrying that exact Accept-Language, is essentially never benign.

    The DMPTC callback is the headline of the follow-up. satlo.REDACTED.gov.vn sits on a Vietnamese government domain belonging to the Vietnam Disaster Management Policy and Technology Center, a public service unit under the Vietnam Disaster Management Authority (VNDMA), tracing structurally up to the Ministry of Agriculture and Environment. Public descriptions of DMPTC name its work as satellite data exchange during disasters, remote sensing and GIS for disaster prevention, and DRR (disaster risk reduction) project databases. The subdomain satlo fits the satellite-logging / location activity named in their public profile.     Attribution (Brief)¶

    Chinese-speaking e-crime crew, not state-nexus, on high confidence. The short-form evidence:

    • Accept-Language: zh-CN,zh;q=0.9,en;q=0.8 hard-coded in the malleable profile
    • .aggressor.prop CS client preferences timestamped Tue Nov 17 12:47:37 CST 2026 (Asia/Shanghai working hours)
    • Operator notes, folder names, and PowerShell transcripts in Simplified Chinese
    • Distribution archive literally named ARTEX菠菜专武（带武器库）.7z, meaning "ARTEX gambling-industry special-weapon (with weapons library)"
    • The teamserver image is the public October 2023 Cobalt Strike 4.9 crack that has circulated on Chinese security forums since late 2023, not a bespoke build
    • A dedicated payout_analyst AI agent in the ARTEX-SPIN fork (出金链路分析员, "cash-out chain analyst") designed to map TRC20 withdrawal paths of illegal gambling sites
    • Teamserver-side logs for 2026-11-20 show beacon traffic arriving from 213.33.190[.]0/24 and 195.239.51[.]0/24. The second /24 is the same block the nikitin and maksimov beacons sit in on the main roster, which reclassifies that pair from "Russia-geo residential victims" to operator-controlled test machines or a shared-VPN exit the operator uses alongside the primary 45.251.240[.]174 teamserver.
    • The teamserver's own events.log catalogues at least 17 named operator accounts. One (neo) is the human operator joined 2026-11-17 12:47:37 CST and remained connected throughout capture, with no corresponding has left entry. The other 16 are headless agscript operator accounts, each tied to a specific Aggressor automation script: tsop1 (polling / health-check, hundreds of 25-second joins over the capture window), t5986 / t5987 / t443 (listener-test scripts), g443 (payload generator, sibling of the gen5985.cna file on disk), noteop1..9 + noteadm / noteadm2..4 + notefin + noteread (fifteen beacon-note scripts corresponding to the operator's own addnotes*.cna / batchnotes*.sh files), plus oneN / rdN / rdN2 / finrd (a paired loop batch). Automation infrastructure of this depth is uncommon in opportunistic e-crime. This is a production workbench maintained over time.
    • Working-hours fingerprint from the tsop1 polling script in events.log: continuous activity from 12:28 to 23:19 CST on 2026-11-17 (an eleven-hour noon-to-midnight day), followed by a midnight automation window (00:09 to 01:09 CST on 2026-11-18) when the note-management scripts ran in sequence. Timing is consistent with the Shanghai-time locale already established in .aggressor.prop.
    • Operator RDP source IPs geolocate to Guangdong province, China, with direct China Telecom / China Mobile broadband on 53 of ~55 sessions. See Operator Geolocation section below.

    Operator Geolocation: What the TerminalServices Log Burned¶

    The teamserver host's own log was also published on the opendir. It records every session logon and reconnect with the source IP of the connecting party, exactly the artefact where this kind of operator most reliably slips. The log contains over 50 rows and one Chinese string 本地 ("Local") in the column header confirming that the system locale on the operator's box is Chinese.

    Conclusion¶

    An operator reflex, binding a quick file-serving script to the wrong directory, handed me the complete working memory of their Cobalt Strike 4.9 teamserver, their Sliver operator config, and the Aggressor script in which they had catalogued every active victim in their own shorthand. Thirteen beacons on the headline roster, two confirmed Vietnamese government victims, one cluster of five Brazilian municipal governments on a shared Fiorilli SIPWEB product. VNCERT and CERT.br were notified ahead of publication.
